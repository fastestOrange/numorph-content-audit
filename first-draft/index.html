Big AI models were never built for the edge

01

They need hardware you don't have

Large models assume datacenter silicon: GPUs, power, and cooling that a factory floor, a warehouse robot, or a drone cannot carry.

02

They charge you for stillness

Inference cost scales with frames, not with events. An empty aisle at 30 fps costs exactly as much to process as a busy one.

03

They don't scale down

A large model is one fixed size. You cannot run a tenth of it on a small job, so every camera pays the full price regardless of how little is happening.

04

They answer too late

Sending perception to the cloud and waiting for a reply adds latency a moving machine cannot absorb. By the time the answer arrives, the scene has changed.

Applying ERN-AI to warehouse cameras

Thirty identical pictures a secondversusonly when something moves

The redundancy is created at the camera. That is where it has to be removed.

Every frame, whether or not anything happened

A camera running at 30 fps in a static warehouse aisle transmits thirty nearly identical images every second. The information content changes only when something moves: everything else is duplicated pixels consuming bandwidth, inference cycles, and disk. In dim aisles it sees nothing at all without an expensive infrared retrofit: added illuminators, added hardware, added maintenance.

Only what changed, extracted at the sensor

ERN-AI is a perception-native compute layer that sits between the sensor and the AI stack, converting continuous frame data into sparse event representations before it ever reaches the network or the GPU. Redundancy is removed at the point of capture, where it originates. Motion detection and classification hold below 5 lux with no infrared illuminators fitted.
